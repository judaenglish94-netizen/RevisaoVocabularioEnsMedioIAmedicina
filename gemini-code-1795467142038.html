Text: How Artificial Intelligence is Transforming Modern Medicine CEFR B1

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is rapidly changing the way doctors and hospitals work around the world. One of the biggest benefits of this technology is its ability to improve medical diagnosis. By analyzing thousands of X-rays, MRIs, and CT scans in just a few seconds, AI algorithms can detect tiny abnormalities that the human eye might miss. For example, studies published in the scientific journal Nature showed that AI systems can assist radiologists in breast cancer screening, helping to reduce both false positives and false negatives.

Beyond medical imaging, AI is also accelerating the discovery of new drugs and personalized treatment plans. A major breakthrough occurred when Google DeepMind's AlphaFold predicted the 3D structures of more than 200 million proteins, a task that previously took scientists years to complete for just a single protein. This achievement helps researchers understand how a specific disease develops and how to design better medications for each patient based on their genetic profile.

The adoption of AI tools in hospitals is already a proven reality, not just a future idea. According to the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), more than 950 AI and machine-learning-enabled medical devices had been authorized for clinical use by 2024. These tools are used in cardiology, neurology, and emergency rooms to monitor vital signs, predict complications before severe symptoms appear, and improve overall accuracy in hospital care.

Despite these impressive advances, experts agree that AI will not replace human doctors. Instead, it works as a powerful assistant that reduces administrative tasks and gives professionals more time to focus on human care. However, healthcare systems must still address important challenges, such as protecting sensitive medical data and ensuring that AI algorithms are fair and unbiased for all populations.
